## Neer (proximal humerus)

**Originator:** Charles Neer, 1970 (revised).
**Structure:** Counts **displaced parts** (≥1 cm displacement OR ≥45° angulation) among the four anatomical segments: greater tuberosity, lesser tuberosity, surgical neck, articular surface (head).
**Categories:** 1-part (minimally displaced regardless of fracture lines), 2-part, 3-part, 4-part, fracture-dislocation, head-splitting.

**What it predicts:** prognosis (especially AVN risk in 4-part), and operative vs non-operative threshold.

**Clinical relevance:** Despite imperfect inter-observer reliability, Neer remains the dominant clinical system. 4-part fractures, especially in elderly patients, raise the question of arthroplasty (hemi- or reverse total shoulder).

**Pitfalls:**
- The 1cm / 45° threshold is interpreted variably between observers.
- Valgus-impacted 4-part patterns may have preserved blood supply and behave better than other 4-part patterns (Hertel criteria).

## Mason (radial head)

**Originator:** M.L. Mason, 1954.
**Structure:**
- **Type I:** Non-displaced or minimally displaced (<2mm)
- **Type II:** Displaced (>2mm) without comminution; mechanical block possible
- **Type III:** Comminuted, requires excision or replacement
- **Type IV (Johnston modification):** Any of the above with elbow dislocation

**What it predicts:** treatment pathway (non-op, ORIF, replacement) and risk of associated injuries.

**Clinical relevance:** Mason II with a mechanical block to forearm rotation typically goes to ORIF; Mason III often goes to radial head replacement; the "terrible triad" (Mason + coronoid + dislocation) needs combined reconstruction.

## Schatzker (tibial plateau)

**Originator:** Joseph Schatzker, 1979.
**Structure:** Six types (I–VI):

- **I:** Lateral split (no depression) — typical in young patients with good bone
- **II:** Lateral split + depression — typical in older bone
- **III:** Lateral pure depression
- **IV:** Medial plateau (split, depression, or both) — high-energy
- **V:** Bicondylar — both plateaus
- **VI:** Plateau + metadiaphyseal dissociation — the highest-energy pattern

**What it predicts:** treatment approach, implant strategy (medial plate vs lateral plate vs both vs ex-fix), and associated soft-tissue injury risk.

**Clinical relevance:**
- Schatzker IV: always look for popliteal vessel and peroneal nerve injury — it's effectively a knee dislocation equivalent.
- Schatzker V/VI: high rate of compartment syndrome.
- The posteromedial fragment (a separate concept from the six-type system) is often invisible on AP and dictates approach.

**Pitfalls:**
- Posteromedial fragment is the classical missed finding — always read the lateral and get CT.
- Schatzker doesn't capture posterolateral fragments well — Luo's three-column concept complements it.

## Lauge-Hansen + Weber (ankle)

### Lauge-Hansen — mechanism-based
**Originator:** Niels Lauge-Hansen, 1948.
**Structure:** Two-word naming convention: foot position at injury + force direction.
- **Supination–Adduction (SA)** I–II
- **Supination–External Rotation (SER)** I–IV — the most common pattern
- **Pronation–Abduction (PA)** I–III
- **Pronation–External Rotation (PER)** I–IV

Each stage describes the sequential disruption of structures around the ankle. SER IV (the most advanced SER stage) includes spiral lateral malleolus + posterior malleolus + medial malleolus or deltoid disruption — the "complete" SER pattern.

**What it predicts:** sequence of injury, expected associated injuries, mechanism for surgical reconstruction.

### Weber / Danis-Weber — anatomic
**Structure:** Based on the level of the fibular fracture relative to the syndesmosis:
- **Weber A:** below syndesmosis (infrasyndesmotic)
- **Weber B:** at the level of syndesmosis (transsyndesmotic)
- **Weber C:** above syndesmosis (suprasyndesmotic) — by definition implies syndesmotic disruption

**What it predicts:** syndesmotic involvement, operative threshold.

**Clinical relevance:** the two systems are complementary — Lauge-Hansen for the mechanism and sequence; Weber for the anatomy and the syndesmotic question. **Maisonneuve fracture** (proximal fibula + syndesmosis + medial structures) is the high-energy PER pattern.

## Hawkins (talar neck)

**Originator:** Lyle Hawkins, 1970.
**Structure:** Four types, each predicting AVN risk based on disruption of the talus's tenuous blood supply:
- **Type I:** Non-displaced — AVN ~10%
- **Type II:** Subtalar dislocation — AVN ~30%
- **Type III:** Subtalar AND ankle dislocation — AVN ~80%
- **Type IV (Canale modification):** Type III + talonavicular dislocation — AVN approaching 100%

**Hawkins sign:** subchondral lucency in the talar dome at 6–8 weeks indicates preserved blood supply (a positive sign — paradoxically, the lucency indicates revascularisation).

**Clinical relevance:** all Hawkins II and above are urgent — reduction protects the remaining blood supply.

## Sanders (calcaneus)

**Originator:** Roy Sanders, 1992.
**Structure:** Based on **CT (coronal view)** — the number of articular fragments at the widest aspect of the posterior facet.
- **Type I:** Non-displaced (regardless of number of fracture lines)
- **Type II:** Two articular fragments (IIa, IIb, IIc by location of the line)
- **Type III:** Three articular fragments (IIIab, IIIac, IIIbc)
- **Type IV:** Four or more articular fragments (highly comminuted)

**What it predicts:** outcome after ORIF (worse with higher type), threshold for primary subtalar arthrodesis.

**Clinical relevance:** **Sanders cannot be applied without CT.** Plain film is insufficient. Type IV in many centres goes to primary subtalar arthrodesis rather than ORIF.

- [ ] Worked examples for II / III / IV

## Letournel-Judet (acetabulum)

**Originator:** Emile Letournel and Robert Judet, 1964 onward.
**Structure:** Ten patterns divided into:

*Elementary (5):*
1. Posterior wall
2. Posterior column
3. Anterior wall
4. Anterior column
5. Transverse

*Associated (5):*
6. Posterior column + posterior wall
7. Transverse + posterior wall
8. T-shaped
9. Anterior + posterior hemitransverse
10. Both-column

**What it predicts:** surgical approach (Kocher-Langenbeck, ilioinguinal, anterior intrapelvic, extended iliofemoral, combined), feasibility of fixation, prognosis.

**Imaging:** AP + obturator oblique + iliac oblique (Judet views) + CT with 3D reconstruction. The six radiographic lines (iliopectineal, ilioischial, anterior rim, posterior rim, roof, teardrop) are interpreted to deduce the pattern.

**Clinical relevance:** complex; surgeons in this field train specifically for it. The both-column fracture is the most common pattern.

- [ ] Surgical approach mapping
- [ ] Marginal impaction concept

- [ ] Posterior wall percentage and stability concept

## Tile + Young-Burgess (pelvic ring)

### Tile (A/B/C)
- **A:** Posterior arch intact (stable)
- **B:** Posterior arch incomplete (rotationally unstable, vertically stable)
- **C:** Posterior arch complete (rotationally and vertically unstable)

Mirrors the AO/OTA 61 type letters.

### Young-Burgess (mechanism-based)
- **Lateral Compression (LC) I–III:** side-to-side force
- **Anteroposterior Compression (APC) I–III:** open-book mechanism
- **Vertical Shear (VS):** vertical translation, very unstable
- **Combined Mechanism (CM):** features of more than one

**What it predicts:**
- **Tile:** stability and operative threshold.
- **Young-Burgess:** associated soft-tissue injury and resuscitation needs (APC III and VS have the highest mortality from haemorrhage).

## Distal radius — Frykman, Fernandez, Melone

### Frykman (8-type)
Based on intra-articular involvement (radiocarpal joint, distal radioulnar joint) and presence of ulnar styloid fracture. Types 1–8.

### Fernandez (mechanism-based, 5 types)
- I: Bending (Colles', Smith)
- II: Shearing (Barton — volar or dorsal)
- III: Compression of articular surface (die-punch)
- IV: Avulsion (radial styloid, dorsal ulnar)
- V: Combined / high-energy

### Melone (4-part articular)
For complex intra-articular distal radius fractures: the four key fragments are radial shaft, radial styloid, dorsomedial (lunate facet), and volar-medial (lunate facet).

**Clinical relevance:** Fernandez is mechanism-based and intuitive for surgical planning. Melone identifies the lunate facet fragments that drive volar locking plate strategies. Frykman is the registry historical workhorse.
