## Segment 41 — Tibial plateau (proximal tibia)

**Scope:** the proximal tibial articular surface and the metaphyseal region.

**Type letters in segment 41:**
- **41-A** — Extra-articular
- **41-B** — Partial articular
- **41-C** — Complete articular

**Key complementary classifications:**
- **Schatzker** (I–VI) — the dominant clinical system for tibial plateau:
  - I: Lateral split
  - II: Lateral split + depression
  - III: Lateral pure depression
  - IV: Medial plateau
  - V: Bicondylar
  - VI: Plateau + metadiaphyseal dissociation
- **Luo's three-column concept** — lateral, medial, and posterior columns; aids posterior approach planning especially for posteromedial fragments.

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Medial plateau (Schatzker IV)** is a high-energy injury — always look for concomitant ligamentous and neurovascular injury (popliteal vessels, peroneal nerve).
- **Posteromedial fragment** — invisible on AP, easily missed without lateral and CT. Often requires a separate posterior approach.
- **Compartment syndrome** is a recognised risk, especially in Schatzker V/VI patterns.

**Imaging needs:** AP, lateral, oblique; **CT essentially mandatory** for B-type and C-type plateau fractures.

## Segment 42 — Tibial shaft (and fibula)

**Scope:** below the tibial tuberosity to above the supra-malleolar region.

**Type letters in segment 42:**
- **42-A** — Simple (spiral, oblique, or transverse — one fracture line)
- **42-B** — Wedge
- **42-C** — Multifragmentary / complex

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Open tibial fractures** are common (the subcutaneous medial border is exposed in any high-energy mechanism). Always grade with Gustilo-Anderson.
- **Compartment syndrome** — the tibial shaft fracture is the classical "compartment syndrome until proven otherwise" injury. Document compartment status repeatedly.
- **Concomitant fibula fracture** — describe separately. Intact fibula with displaced tibia behaves differently mechanically (the fibula acts as a strut, creating valgus or varus deformity depending on the level).

**Imaging needs:** AP and lateral including knee and ankle. Stress views for distal tibia/syndesmosis if implicated.

## Segment 43 — Distal tibia (pilon / plafond)

**Scope:** the distal tibial metaphysis and articular plafond.

**Type letters in segment 43:**
- **43-A** — Extra-articular (above the joint line)
- **43-B** — Partial articular
- **43-C** — Complete articular

**Key complementary classifications:**
- **Rüedi-Allgöwer** (I–III) — historically influential, simpler 3-grade system.

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Soft tissue status dominates everything.** The classical mistake is to operate before the soft tissues have settled. Most pilon fractures get **staged management**: external fixator first, then ORIF days to weeks later.
- **Posterior malleolar fragment** assessment requires CT — easy to miss on plain film.
- **Articular impaction** — die-punch areas are often invisible without CT.

**Imaging needs:** AP, lateral, mortise; **CT essentially mandatory** for all B-type and C-type.

## Segment 44 — Ankle (malleolar)

**Scope:** lateral malleolus, medial malleolus, posterior malleolus, syndesmosis.

**Type letters in segment 44 (based on the fibular fracture relative to the syndesmosis):**
- **44-A** — Infrasyndesmotic (fibula below the syndesmosis — Weber A equivalent)
- **44-B** — Transsyndesmotic (fibula at the level of the syndesmosis — Weber B equivalent)
- **44-C** — Suprasyndesmotic (fibula above the syndesmosis — Weber C equivalent)

**Key complementary classifications:**
- **Lauge-Hansen** (mechanism-based: supination-adduction, supination-external rotation, pronation-abduction, pronation-external rotation) — describes the stages of injury propagation.
- **Weber / Danis-Weber** (A, B, C) — anatomic, based on fibular level relative to syndesmosis.

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Syndesmotic instability** — must be assessed on stress views or intra-operatively. A Weber B with syndesmotic instability behaves like a Weber C.
- **Posterior malleolus** size and morphology — increasingly the topic of separate classification systems (Haraguchi, Bartoníček-Rammelt).
- **Maisonneuve fracture** — proximal fibular fracture with disrupted syndesmosis. Always palpate and image the proximal fibula if the ankle injury seems disproportionate to the distal findings.

**Imaging needs:** AP, lateral, **mortise** (essential for syndesmosis). Full-length tib-fib if Maisonneuve suspected.
