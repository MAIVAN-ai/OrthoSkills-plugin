## Segment 32 — Femoral shaft

**Scope:** below the lesser trochanter (subtrochanteric region included) to above the supracondylar region.

**Type letters in segment 32:**
- **32-A** — Simple (single fracture line, one of: spiral, oblique, transverse)
- **32-B** — Wedge (one intermediate fragment, ends remain in contact after reduction)
- **32-C** — Multifragmentary / complex (no end-to-end contact after reduction)

**Subtrochanteric extension** is a critical sub-feature: any fracture line involving the area from the lesser trochanter down ~5cm is biomechanically subtrochanteric and demands extra attention to reduction quality and implant length.

**Key complementary classifications:**
- **Winquist-Hansen** for comminution grading (I–IV) — useful for stability assessment.
- For open injuries: **Gustilo-Anderson** (I, II, IIIA/B/C).

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Atypical femoral fractures** (AFF) in bisphosphonate users — transverse line on the lateral cortex, beaking, often bilateral. Always ask about long-term bisphosphonates and consider DXA + contralateral imaging.
- **Reverse oblique subtrochanteric** patterns deform predictably (proximal fragment in flexion, abduction, external rotation — pulled by iliopsoas, gluteus medius, short external rotators). Reduction technique matters more than implant choice.
- **Floating knee** — concomitant ipsilateral tibial shaft fracture changes the surgical priority.

**Imaging needs:** AP and lateral including hip and knee. CT not usually needed for shaft.

## Segment 33 — Distal femur

**Scope:** supracondylar and intra-articular distal femur.

**Type letters in segment 33:**
- **33-A** — Extra-articular (supracondylar — above the joint line)
- **33-B** — Partial articular (one condyle, the other intact)
- **33-C** — Complete articular (both condyles + intra-articular involvement)

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Periprosthetic distal femur** fractures (around a knee replacement) — extremely important sub-context that changes implant options entirely (retrograde nail vs locking plate; Su, Lewis, or Rorabeck classification for periprosthetic patterns).
- **Hoffa fracture** — coronal-plane partial articular fracture of the posterior condyle. Easy to miss on lateral; CT is the diagnostic study.
- The fracture line direction (sagittal vs coronal vs oblique) affects implant choice — coronal fractures need anterior-to-posterior screws.

**Imaging needs:** AP and lateral; CT routine for any 33-B or 33-C.

## Segment 34 — Patella

**Scope:** all patella fractures.

**Type letters in segment 34:**
- **34-A** — Extra-articular (avulsion patterns of the upper or lower pole)
- **34-B** — Partial articular (vertical pattern, lateral or medial fragment)
- **34-C** — Complete articular (transverse, comminuted, stellate)

**Critical question — extensor mechanism integrity:**
The classification matters less than the clinical question: **can the patient actively extend the knee against gravity?** A non-displaced fracture with intact extensor mechanism may be managed non-operatively; any disruption of extensor continuity is typically operative.

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Bipartite patella** is a normal variant — usually superolateral, with smooth corticated edges. Don't classify it as a fracture.
- **Patellar sleeve avulsion** in paediatric patients can look benign on plain film — the cartilage is not radio-opaque.

**Imaging needs:** AP, lateral, and skyline / sunrise. CT for comminuted 34-C if surgical planning needs it.
