## Segment 11 — Proximal humerus

**Scope:** humeral head, anatomical neck, surgical neck, greater tuberosity, lesser tuberosity, and the surrounding bone above the surgical neck.

**Type letters in segment 11:**
- **11-A** — Extra-articular, two-part (involving surgical neck, greater tuberosity, or lesser tuberosity)
- **11-B** — Extra-articular, three-part
- **11-C** — Articular / anatomical neck / four-part / head-splitting

**Key downstream classification:** **Neer** (4-part system: greater tuberosity, lesser tuberosity, surgical neck, articular surface — counts displaced parts). See `skills/05-region-classifications/references/`.

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- Distinguishing two-part surgical neck from three-part requires looking at all tuberosities individually.
- Head-splitting fractures (11-C) and four-part fractures in elderly patients raise the question of AVN and arthroplasty.
- The axillary nerve runs immediately below — always document neurovascular status.

**Imaging needs:** True AP, scapular Y, and axillary views. CT routine for any three-part or four-part suspicion.

## Segment 12 — Humeral shaft

**Scope:** below the surgical neck to above the supracondylar region.

**Type letters in segment 12:**
- **12-A** — Simple (one fracture line, two main fragments)
- **12-B** — Wedge (intermediate fragment, but ends in contact after reduction)
- **12-C** — Multifragmentary / complex (no contact between proximal and distal ends after reduction)

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Radial nerve palsy** is the classic associated injury (especially distal-third spiral fractures — Holstein-Lewis). Always document wrist and finger extension and dorsum-of-hand sensation pre- and post-reduction.
- Pathological fractures are common in this segment — always consider the possibility.
- Most are managed non-operatively in a functional brace — a critical exception to the otherwise surgical bias of AO/OTA-coded injuries.

**Imaging needs:** AP and lateral, including the joint above and the joint below. CT not usually required for shaft.

## Segment 13 — Distal humerus

**Scope:** the distal humerus above and through the articular surface — supracondylar, intra-articular, partial articular.

**Type letters in segment 13:**
- **13-A** — Extra-articular (supracondylar — above the joint line)
- **13-B** — Partial articular (one column intact, the other involved — e.g. capitellum, trochlea)
- **13-C** — Complete articular (both columns + intra-articular involvement)

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- The "Y" pattern (intercondylar T or Y fracture) is the classic complete articular 13-C.
- Always assess the ulnar nerve at the cubital tunnel.
- In children, the AO paediatric extensions apply (supracondylar humerus fractures and the Gartland classification).

**Imaging needs:** AP and lateral, often with traction views. CT routine for any partial- or complete-articular pattern.
