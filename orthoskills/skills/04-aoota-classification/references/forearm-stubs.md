## Segment 21 — Proximal forearm

**Scope:** proximal radius (radial head and neck) and proximal ulna (olecranon, coronoid).

**Type letters in segment 21:**
- **21-A** — Extra-articular fractures of one bone (ulna OR radius)
- **21-B** — Intra-articular fracture of one bone
- **21-C** — Intra-articular fracture of both bones

**Key downstream classifications:**
- **Mason** (radial head — Type I non-displaced, II displaced, III comminuted, IV with dislocation)
- **Mayo** or **Schatzker olecranon** classifications for olecranon patterns
- **Regan-Morrey** for coronoid fractures

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **"Terrible triad"** — radial head fracture + coronoid fracture + elbow dislocation. Easy to under-classify if not all three are appreciated.
- **Monteggia-equivalent** lesions — proximal ulna fracture with radial head dislocation. Always look at the radiocapitellar line on lateral.

## Segment 22 — Forearm shaft

**Scope:** radial and ulnar shafts.

**Type letters in segment 22:**
- **22-A** — Simple (single fracture line, of one or both bones)
- **22-B** — Wedge (one or both bones)
- **22-C** — Complex / multifragmentary

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Monteggia** — fracture of the proximal third of the ulna + radial head dislocation.
- **Galeazzi** — fracture of the distal third of the radius + distal radio-ulnar joint disruption.
- Both must be explicitly described in addition to the AO/OTA code — they have specific eponymous classification systems (Bado for Monteggia).
- Adult both-bone forearm fractures are surgical by default (unlike the humeral shaft).

## Segment 23 — Distal radius (and distal ulna)

**Scope:** distal radius from the metaphyseal flare to the articular surface; distal ulna styloid and head.

**Type letters in segment 23:**
- **23-A** — Extra-articular
- **23-B** — Partial articular
- **23-C** — Complete articular

**Key downstream classifications:**
- **Frykman** (8-type system based on intra-articular involvement and ulnar styloid)
- **Fernandez** (mechanism-based: bending, shearing, compression, avulsion, combined)
- **Melone** (for complex intra-articular patterns — the four-part concept)

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Colles' vs Smith's vs Barton's** — descriptive eponyms still in common use; map to AO/OTA codes but don't replace them.
- **Volar Barton** (volar shear, partial articular) → almost always operative.
- **Die-punch** / **lunate facet fragment** — frequently missed on AP, requires careful lateral and often CT.
- **DRUJ instability** — always assess and document.

**Imaging needs:** PA and lateral; oblique helpful; CT for any C-type or articular step-off > 2mm.
