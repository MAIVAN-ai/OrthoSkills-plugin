## Spine (AOSpine systems)

The AO/OTA Compendium adopts the **AOSpine** classification systems for spine, which evolved separately from the long-bone Compendium structure.

### Upper cervical spine (occipital condyles → C2)

**Key systems:**
- **Anderson-D'Alonzo** for odontoid (C2 dens) fractures (Type I tip, II base, III through C2 body)
- **Hangman's** classification (Levine-Edwards I–IV) for traumatic spondylolisthesis of C2
- **Jefferson** for C1 ring (atlas) burst fractures, with Dickman classification for transverse atlantal ligament integrity

### Subaxial cervical spine (C3–C7)

**Key system: AOSpine Subaxial Cervical**
Two-level structure: morphology (A compression, B distraction, C translation) + facet involvement (F0–F4).

**Companion: SLIC** (Subaxial Cervical Spine Injury Classification) — incorporates morphology, disco-ligamentous integrity, and neurological status; gives a treatment-relevant score.

### Thoracolumbar spine (T1–L5)

**Key system: AOSpine TL** — three-level structure: morphology (A, B, C) + neurological status (N0–N4) + modifiers (M1, M2).

**Companion: TLICS** (Thoracolumbar Injury Classification and Severity Score) — sums morphology + posterior ligamentous complex (PLC) + neurological status into a score that informs operative vs non-operative decision.

**Clinical pitfalls (spine, general):**
- **Always check the neurological status** — every spinal fracture classification requires it.
- **Cauda equina is a surgical emergency** — saddle anaesthesia, urinary retention, bilateral leg weakness.
- **CT is the workhorse imaging** for fracture characterisation; **MRI** is essential for PLC and cord assessment.
- **Burst fracture with retropulsion** — the retropulsed fragment can cause cord compression; correlate with neurological status.

## Segment 61 — Pelvic ring

**Scope:** anterior (pubic symphysis, pubic rami) and posterior (sacroiliac joint, sacrum, iliac wing) elements of the pelvic ring.

**AO/OTA type letters for 61:**
- **61-A** — Posterior arch intact (anterior injury only)
- **61-B** — Posterior arch incomplete (rotationally unstable, vertically stable)
- **61-C** — Posterior arch complete (rotationally AND vertically unstable)

**Key complementary classifications:**
- **Tile** (A/B/C) — closely parallels the AO/OTA structure; widely used.
- **Young-Burgess** — mechanism-based: lateral compression (LC), anteroposterior compression (APC), vertical shear (VS), combined mechanism (CM). More predictive of associated soft-tissue injury and resuscitation strategy.

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Haemodynamic instability** — pelvic ring fractures, especially APC III and VS, can cause life-threatening retroperitoneal haemorrhage. **The pelvic binder goes on before the X-ray.**
- **Posterior element injury determines stability.** Anterior-only injuries are usually stable; any posterior involvement requires careful assessment.
- **Sacral fracture morphology** — Denis zones I (lateral), II (foraminal), III (central) predict neurological injury risk.
- **Open pelvic fractures** are catastrophic — combined orthopaedic, vascular, urological, and general surgical involvement.

**Imaging needs:** AP pelvis, inlet, outlet; **CT mandatory** for full characterisation. CT angiography if active bleeding suspected.

## Segment 62 — Acetabulum

**Scope:** the acetabular columns, walls, and roof.

**AO/OTA type letters for 62:**
- **62-A** — Partial articular (one column / one wall)
- **62-B** — Partial articular (transverse-type patterns)
- **62-C** — Both-column fractures

**Dominant complementary classification: Letournel-Judet (10 patterns):**

*Elementary patterns:*
1. Posterior wall
2. Posterior column
3. Anterior wall
4. Anterior column
5. Transverse

*Associated patterns:*
6. Posterior column + posterior wall
7. Transverse + posterior wall
8. T-shaped
9. Anterior + posterior hemitransverse
10. Both-column

**Clinical pitfalls:**
- **Pattern recognition on plain film is hard.** The Letournel system uses three views (AP, obturator oblique, iliac oblique), and CT with 3D reconstruction is almost always needed for definitive classification.
- **Marginal impaction** of the posterior wall changes treatment options significantly.
- **Hip dislocation** is a common associated injury — reduce urgently.
- **Sciatic nerve assessment** is mandatory.

**Imaging needs:** AP, obturator oblique, iliac oblique (Judet views); **CT with 3D reconstruction mandatory** for any surgical planning.
